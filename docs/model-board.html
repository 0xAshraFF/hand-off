Why the evidence label is the product

Every comparison site shows numbers. None of them tell you when they ran out of numbers and started guessing.

Three labels, and they're the reason to trust the rest. Benchmark-backed means a public leaderboard measures this job directly and the ranking is its ranking. Split decision means two credible benchmarks disagree, and the page shows both rather than picking the flattering one. Hands-on only means nothing public measures this and the pick is somebody's judgement, labelled as such. A recommendation engine that never admits the third case is one you cannot calibrate against — and the third case is where your hardest-won knowledge actually lives.
